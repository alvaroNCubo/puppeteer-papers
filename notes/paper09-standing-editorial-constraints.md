# Paper 9 — standing editorial constraints

Recorded 2026-07-26, at the point the paper was judged ready. These are decisions taken *against*
plausible improvements, so a later session does not helpfully undo them.

## Do not develop §6 further

§3–§4–§6 (client, present/past, recognition) answer the paper's **second** question, and that
question has one status only: **what the first question needs in order to be legible at all** — an
invariance nobody can observe is not a result. It is not a second parallel contribution.

§6 is already dense enough — act granularity, the wrong-correlation failure, record evolution — that
a reader can mistake the paper for an attempt at the definitive paper on recognition. The text denies
it in several places and that is judged sufficient. The correct action is therefore **no action**:
do not add, do not deepen, do not develop. If recognition needs more, it needs a paper.

Guard on this: §1 now states the second question's subordinate status explicitly, and §10 lists
*who is entitled to read a routine* among the questions left standing. If either of those weakens,
the subordination is gone.

## Do not restore the material parked in notes

Three subjects were cut from this paper on purpose and live in notes, each with its evidence:

- domain growth and evolution → [[future-paper-domain-growth]] (§8.2 keeps only the two examples
  that answer the threat to the identity claim; the ladder, verb retirement and split/merge practice
  are **out**)
- an incomplete record answers plausibly → [[future-paper-incomplete-records-answer-plausibly]]
- a fact must be derivable within one actor → [[future-paper-a-fact-must-belong-to-one-actor]]

The last two remain in §8.4 at about 250 and 200 words, subordinated, with "neither is the result of
this paper". Do not re-expand them; a reviewer asked for that expansion once and a second reader
showed why it costs the thesis.

## Relocating detail to the appendix is measured net-negative

Tried twice and reverted: moving §8.5's three symptoms and §6's Lab H findings to Appendix A moved
303 words in and took only 143 out, leaving the paper 160 words longer with the same content in two
places. §2 worked (2,414 → 1,295) **because it had genuine redundancy** — eight demonstrations of
one thing. Where the detail *is* the content, a pointer costs what it saves. Cut or keep; do not
relocate.

## The abstract stays at ~270 words, not 200

Reaching 200 means deleting one of three things each of which an earlier round asked for in writing:
the contribution-type sentence, the two limits, or the clause deriving the stand-ins / public
surface / admissible state from the three declared ports. 200 is a venue ceiling; this series' own
abstracts run longer, and the author has been explicit that readability comes before conformance.

## Two constructs, one definition each

*Identity* is defined in §5, *closure* in §9 (where the obligations table is). Everywhere else uses
the word. The closure gloss — a port is an obligation the domain declared and left open — belongs in
§9 alone; it was in four places once. And both carry the same discipline sentence: the counts are
checkable, the name is a reading of them.
